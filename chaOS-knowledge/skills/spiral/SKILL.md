# The spiral

Contract: [CONTRACT.md](../../CONTRACT.md). Words: [LEXICON.md](../../LEXICON.md).

| Step | Name | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Intention | What does the person want? Open with `intent: <one sentence>`. |
| 2 | Direction | What would a good outcome look like, and what kind of truth will it hold? |
| 3 | Context | What is here already: nodes, sources, the dictionary, and what `spiral memory` has learned? |
| 4 | Dance | Work with the person in small steps. Propose; don't interrogate. |
| 5 | Governance | Once something runs, hold it: visible, checked, correctable. Name who decides. |
| 6 | Re-seed | Carry what was learned into the next intent. Name one open question. |

**Core rule:** without repetition, a dance suffices. With repetition, a system emerges, and repetition is what makes governance necessary.

**Knowledge rule:** repetition earns structure, never truth. When a relation grows heavy, it may become a template, a skill or a node. Its weight is never an argument for that node's `status`.

## At each growth stage

| Stage | Do |
|---|---|
| dance | Nothing to build. |
| noticed | Name the pattern to the person, citing the events. |
| routine | Propose a template, skill or node. Do not create it unasked. |
| governed | Make it checkable: a contract rule, a test, an audit row. |
| re-seed | Use it in new work, and watch where it no longer fits. |

## Variation check

When the hook asks, name what changed that the structure did not plan for, before you follow the pattern. A kind of item that no longer fits its type is a stop-the-line signal, not an inconvenience.

## Reflection

When reflection is due, offer to run `spiral reflect` after the request, then follow the [audit](../audit/SKILL.md) skill.
